PROJECT QUESTION & PITCH

Human direction

The group supplied its topic, the exact research question about post-humanism and future needs of non-human users, two starting URLs (MoMA Magazine and ACM Digital Library), and the name Sweet Chili Heat with members Evan Winther, Steff Siu, Vian Esterhuizen, and Victoria Shaw. The group’s question—not an AI-generated topic—anchors the app.

AI contribution and check

AI organized the pitch into the reading-room framing and added a cybernetics feedback-loop model. The two supplied links could not be bibliographically verified during this work; their titles, authors, dates, and relevance remain for the group to check.

COURSE MATERIALS

Human-provided sources

The group attached eight ARCH 602 PDFs, including the course outline and module handouts on foundational treatises, mathematics and plasticity, modernism, cybernetics and Japanese Metabolists, architectural “isms,” deconstruction, adjacent discourses, and visionary digital intensities. The cybernetics handout names Gordon Pask, Nicholas Negroponte, Christopher Alexander, Reyner Banham, Yona Friedman, and the Metabolists; other handouts list Philip Ball, Sanford Kwinter, and D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson.

AI contribution and check

AI extracted reading-list information and used relevant entries to seed cards. These attachments are course handouts and reading lists, not the full texts of every listed work. Most card lines are therefore labeled as original reading prompts, not quotations. The group should check every citation against the actual reading and its chosen edition.

LITERARY & ECOLOGICAL DIRECTIONS

Human direction

The group asked whether Octavia Butler, Audre Lorde, Douglas Adams, and Terry Pratchett fit, then added Robert Macfarlane’s Is a River Alive? and Robin Wall Kimmerer. These are human-supplied interests and are distinguished in the cards as fiction, critical lenses, or environmental writing—not misrepresented as architectural history.

AI contribution

AI suggested Donna Haraway, María Puig de la Bellacasa, Anna Tsing, and Vinciane Despret as further reading directions. Those suggestions are clearly identified as leads to verify, rather than as sources selected or read by the group.

DESIGN & EDITORIAL DECISIONS

Human direction

The group described a visual card pile, search and taxonomy filters, slide-out reading details, citations, and a public-facing learning goal. It then specified the interaction: selecting one card should lift it from a slightly blurred pile, show three related readings from different categories for cross-pollination, and keep the detail drawer on the right. It explicitly chose not to host PDFs, asking instead for information, quotations, and citations. The group rejected the speculative “Working position” copy and later asked to remove the entire cybernetics configurator panel as confusing.

AI response

The app keeps cards, search, filters, the selected-card view, cross-category recommendations, and the detail drawer; the rejected argument and configurator panel are removed. No PDF upload or storage feature is included. This records the group’s design choices rather than attributing them to AI.

Three documented decisions and corrections

  1. Source ownership: the group supplied the research question, two starting sources, roster, course PDFs, and literary interests. AI used these as the content basis; it did not originate the project topic or group members.
  2. Quote accuracy: course PDFs identify readings but do not provide the full texts of most books. AI did not invent author quotations; cards use labeled reading prompts. The Kimmerer excerpt is short and credited to the publisher’s book page, with the page number left for edition-level verification.
  3. Scope correction: the group rejected assistant-written argument text, asked for no PDF hosting, and then removed the full configurator panel. AI removed those features and kept the site focused on the reading cards and citations.
