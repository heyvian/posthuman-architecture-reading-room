PROJECT QUESTION & PITCH

Human direction

The group supplied its topic, the exact research question about post-humanism and future needs of non-human users, two starting URLs (MoMA Magazine and ACM Digital Library), and the name Sweet Chili Heat with members Evan Winther, Steff Siu, Vian Esterhuizen, and Victoria Shaw. The group’s question—not an AI-generated topic—anchors the app.

AI contribution and check

AI organized the pitch into the reading-room framing and added a cybernetics feedback-loop model. The two supplied links could not be bibliographically verified during this work; their titles, authors, dates, and relevance remain for the group to check.

COURSE MATERIALS

Human-provided sources

The group attached eight ARCH 602 PDFs, including the course outline and module handouts on foundational treatises, mathematics and plasticity, modernism, cybernetics and Japanese Metabolists, architectural “isms,” deconstruction, adjacent discourses, and visionary digital intensities. The cybernetics handout names Gordon Pask, Nicholas Negroponte, Christopher Alexander, Reyner Banham, Yona Friedman, and the Metabolists; other handouts list Philip Ball, Sanford Kwinter, and D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson.

AI contribution and check

AI extracted reading-list information and used relevant entries to seed cards. These attachments are course handouts and reading lists, not the full texts of every listed work. Most card lines are therefore labeled as original reading prompts, not quotations. The group should check every citation against the actual reading and its chosen edition.

The Tafuri, Venturi, Eisenman, and Foucault files did not yield selectable text, so their cards use reading prompts and citations instead of invented quotations.

LITERARY & ECOLOGICAL DIRECTIONS

Human direction

The group proposed Octavia Butler, Audre Lorde, Douglas Adams, and Terry Pratchett; added Robert Macfarlane’s Is a River Alive? and Robin Wall Kimmerer; and identified Kimmerer’s Braiding Sweetgrass, Gathering Moss, and The Serviceberry as valuable sources. The group supplied Diana Beresford-Kroeger’s To Speak for the Trees publisher link, highlighted living ships and bioluminescent university buildings in Binti, and described Adams and Pratchett as sources of critical observations about impersonal or inhuman architecture. These are credited as the group’s ideas; literary prompts are not presented as direct quotations.

AI contribution and editorial judgment

AI added cards that distinguish fiction, critical lenses, and ecological writing from architectural history, and tied each to a specific question about non-human life, scale, reciprocity, or whose needs a system recognizes. Kandinsky’s manifesto, The Creative Act, and Greg Graffin’s “Punk Manifesto” were considered but not added: without a specific, source-supported connection to the research question, including them would risk stretching the scope. Haraway, Puig de la Bellacasa, Tsing, and Despret remain AI-suggested leads, not sources selected by the group.

DESIGN & EDITORIAL DECISIONS

Human direction

The group described a visual card pile, search and taxonomy filters, slide-out reading details, citations, and a public-facing learning goal. It then specified the interaction: selecting one card should lift it from a slightly blurred pile, show three related readings from different categories for cross-pollination, and keep the detail drawer on the right. The group later asked for the pile to shuffle on each page load so the same cards would not always sit on top. It explicitly chose not to host PDFs, asking instead for information, quotations, and citations. The group rejected the speculative “Working position” copy and later asked to remove the entire cybernetics configurator panel as confusing.

AI response

The app keeps cards, search, filters, the selected-card view, cross-category recommendations, and the detail drawer; the pile order is randomized once per page load and remains stable while visitors search or filter. The rejected argument and configurator panel are removed. No PDF upload or storage feature is included. This records the group’s design choices rather than attributing them to AI.

Three documented decisions and corrections

  1. Source ownership: the group supplied the research question, pitch links, roster, course handouts, eighteen unique reading PDFs, the Amsterdam Declaration image, literary interests, and its own interpretive connections. AI used these as the content basis; it did not originate the project topic or those human-provided ideas.
  2. Quote accuracy: verified short excerpts are credited to the supplied text and PDF-page locator; image-only scans are not quoted. The Kimmerer excerpt is credited to the publisher’s book page, with the page number left for edition-level verification. Fiction and books whose full text was not supplied use clearly labeled reading prompts, not fabricated quotations.
  3. Scope correction: the group rejected assistant-written argument text, asked for no PDF hosting, and then removed the full configurator panel. AI removed those features and kept the site focused on the reading cards and citations.
